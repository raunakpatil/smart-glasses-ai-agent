Build plan · v8

Ronie, a hands-free AI assistant for Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2

An Android app, a laptop brain server and a PC companion that together turn Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 glasses into a bilingual assistant. It sees, listens, acts on your devices, coaches interviews, coding and games, and runs free by default.

Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 Android · Kotlin Python brain server PC companion Free by default · paid optional Hindi · English · Hinglish
Talk to it"Hey Ronie" or a tap, in Hindi, English or both mixed.
Set it upKeys, modes, devices and privacy in the companion app.
Hear the answerShort spoken replies in the ear; details on the phone or laptop.
Pay only if you wantFree AI by default; Claude, OpenAI or DeepSeek with your own key.

Hardware we're designing for

Gen 2 has no display, so every response on the glasses is spoken. Anything visual goes to the phone or the laptop.

Glasses hardwareWhat it means for Ronie
No display, open-ear speakersShort spoken cues with a soft chime first. Keep volume low because people nearby can hear the speakers.
12 MP cameraStill photos on demand for reading text. The SDK video stream is lower resolution, so use it only for quick scene questions.
5-mic arrayGood pickup for voice commands and for hearing the person you're talking to.
About 8 h batteryThe camera and a constantly open mic drain it fastest. See the battery section.
Meta Wearables DAT SDK (preview)Camera access plus Bluetooth mic and speakers. Meta keeps the hardware buttons, so Ronie starts from a wake word, a phone tap, a Quick Settings tile or a watch tap.

Sources and scope

Ronie is a fresh codebase that uses three open repos as design references. Their licenses don't allow forking and merging (VisionClaw uses Meta sample terms, OpenGlasses is BSL 1.1, JARVIS has no license).

cutField Assist and clinical recording from OpenGlasses.

Architecture

Realtime voice goes straight from the phone to Gemini Live, so speech has one network hop and the lowest delay. When Gemini calls a tool, the phone forwards it to the brain server on the laptop, which does the heavy work. The companion app is the control panel for all of it.

Connecting the pieces

  • Pairing: the brain server shows a QR code; the companion app scans it once and gets the address and a pairing token. Every later connection is authenticated with that token.
  • Discovery: the app finds the brain on the local network with mDNS, so no IP addresses to type.
  • One settings source: the phone holds all settings and pushes changes to the brain the moment you save them.
  • Graceful degradation: if the brain server is offline, the phone keeps voice, vision, memory and smart-home basics working on its own.

Hindi and English

Ronie understands and speaks Hindi, English and Hinglish (the two mixed in one sentence), and replies the way you spoke. Every mode works in all three.

You: "Ronie, ye kya hai? Iska price batao."
Ronie: "Ye ek Bluetooth speaker hai, JBL Flip 6. Online lagbhag ₹9,000 ka milta hai."
  • Language per turn: detected on every turn, so you can switch mid-conversation.
  • Reply follows you: Hindi in → Hindi out, English in → English out, Hinglish in → Hinglish out. Technical words like "API" or "hashmap" stay in English inside Hindi sentences.
  • Settings in the app: Auto (default), always Hindi, always English or always Hinglish; Devanagari or Roman script for phone text.
  • Voice: a natural Hindi voice and an Indian English voice from the same engine, so mixed sentences don't switch speakers.
  • Indian formats: lakh and crore, ₹, DD/MM dates, Indian phone numbers and addresses.
  • Reading Hindi: Devanagari signs, menus and documents read aloud or translated.
  • Wake words: "Hey Ronie" with any accent, plus "Ronie suno".
  • In each mode: interview practice and cues in the interview's language; coding explanations in your language with code in English; game moves like "Ghoda f3 pe chalo".

Smart home and Android TV

Lights, fans, ACs, plugs and the TV by voice in Hindi or English, all on the local network with free, open-source tools. Devices are added in the companion app.

You: "Ronie, TV pe YouTube kholo aur volume kam karo."
Ronie: "YouTube khol diya, volume 15 pe hai."

Smart home

  • Hub: Home Assistant on the laptop or a Raspberry Pi, so Ronie talks to one API for thousands of device types.
  • Devices: Wi-Fi bulbs and plugs (Wipro, Syska, Tuya / Smart Life with local control), Philips Hue, Matter, and IR for ACs and old TVs through a Broadlink blaster.
  • Scenes: "Movie mode" dims the lights, turns on the TV and sets the AC to 24°. "Good night" turns everything off.
  • Look to control: look at a lamp and say "turn this off"; Ronie matches the photo to the device tagged in that room.
  • Status questions: "Did I leave the geyser on?" reads the state and offers to switch it off.
  • Confirmation before door locks or anything that could spoil food or cause damage.

Android TV / Google TV

  • Remote: power, volume, mute, d-pad, back, home and typing into search through the Android TV Remote protocol (androidtvremote2).
  • Apps: "Open Netflix / YouTube / Hotstar / JioCinema" through deep links, with ADB over the network as a backup.
  • Content: "Play lo-fi on YouTube" or "Play the India match" by casting with pychromecast or searching in the app.
  • What's on screen: look at the TV and ask "who is this actor?".
  • Auto pause: optional pause when you say "main aata hoon" or the doorbell is detected.

Interview mode

Hears the interviewer's question through the glasses mic and answers from your own career data.

1. Career knowledge base

  • Import a resume, LinkedIn export, project write-ups, GitHub READMEs and the job description in the companion app.
  • The AI builds a structured profile: skills, projects, numbers ("cut latency 40%") and STAR stories tagged by theme (conflict, failure, leadership, deadline). The profile is indexed ahead of time so live lookups are instant.
  • Gap check: compares the job description with the profile and lists the questions you're likely to be weak on.

2. Practice mode

  • Ronie plays the interviewer: behavioral, technical or HR rounds, tailored to the job description.
  • Scores each spoken answer on structure, length, filler words and use of numbers, then asks a follow-up.

3. Live answer cues

  • Transcribes the interviewer, sorts the question into a type, and searches the knowledge base.
  • Whispers 2–4 keywords and shows the full answer card on the phone.
  • Uses facts from your data only. When nothing matches, it says so instead of making something up.
Interviewer: "Tell me about a conflict with a teammate."
Ear: "payments API … load test … minus 40 percent"
Phone: Payments API launch · disagreed on retry logic · ran a load test to settle it · errors down 40% · now mentor that dev

4. After the interview

  • Every question asked with your answer, what to improve, and a draft thank-you note, saved in Activity.

Coding copilot

Guides you through a coding problem step by step. The PC companion reads the screen exactly; the glasses camera is the backup for paper and whiteboards.

Problem text + current code + spoken hints
        ▼
Analyzer      problem pattern · edge cases · best complexity
        ▼
Planner       solution split into small numbered steps
        ▼
Step tracker  compares the plan with the typed code, picks the next step
        ▼
Output        glasses: talk track, hints, bug alerts
              laptop overlay: the next 1–3 lines of code
              phone: full solution + complexity summary
  • Talk track first: "Brute force is O(n²). A hashmap of complements gets it to O(n)."
  • Code in chunks: 1–3 lines on the overlay, moving on as soon as the tracker sees them typed.
  • Bug alerts: reads compiler and test errors from the screen ("line 12, off by one, use less-or-equal").
  • Edge cases and complexity summary at the end.
  • Follow-ups: "Can you optimize memory?" starts a new plan.
  • Voice controls: "next", "repeat", "skip", "explain".

Claude Code remote

Give coding tasks to Claude Code on the laptop by voice, approve its actions with a word or a nod, and hear what it did. The PC companion runs Claude Code in headless mode.

You: "Ronie, tell Claude Code to fix the failing login test in the ronie repo."
Ronie: "It wants to run the test suite. Allow?" → "Yes"
Ronie: "Done. Fixed a token expiry check in auth.py, two lines changed, all 48 tests pass. Commit it?"
Voice task → brain server → PC companion
        ▼
claude -p "<task>" --output-format stream-json
       --permission-prompt-tool  (approvals routed to Ronie)
       --resume <session>         (follow-ups keep context)
        ▼
Progress → mission control (live)
Approvals → spoken in the ear → "yes" / "no" / nod
Result → short spoken summary + full diff in the companion app
  • Start, check and follow up: "add a dark mode to mission control", "what's Claude doing?", "also add a test for that".
  • Git by voice: "Commit it" and "Undo that change", always confirmed. Pushing needs a tap in the companion app.
  • Safety: project allowlist set in the app; shell commands always ask; deleting files and force-pushing are blocked; access away from home only through Tailscale.
  • Cost: Claude Code needs a Claude subscription. Without one, the same interface runs Gemini CLI on Google's free tier.

Situational humor

Ronie makes a joke only when the moment has a really good pun, never as a habit.

You, walking past a bakery: "Kya time hua?"
Ronie: "4:30. Perfect bread time, I mean break time."
  • Scored, not forced: with each answer, the AI also suggests one pun about the situation and rates it 1–10, in the same request. Ronie says it only at 9 or above.
  • Answer first: the useful information always comes first; the pun is one short line after it.
  • Cooldown: at most one joke every 30 minutes, and no repeats.
  • Off in serious moments: never during interview cues, coding, sound or safety alerts, medicine reminders, translation, focus mode, bad news, or when you sound stressed.
  • Language-aware: Hindi and Hinglish wordplay when you're speaking Hindi.
  • Learns your taste: "good one" nudges the rate up, "no jokes" turns it off. Off / Rare (default) / Normal in the app.
  • Kind only: about the situation, never about you or people nearby.

Battery

The glasses camera, a mic that's always open over Bluetooth, and constant network use are the biggest drains. Ronie keeps each one off until it's needed and adapts to how much charge is left.

Glasses

  • Camera off by default: one still photo per question instead of a video stream. Short frame bursts (2–3 frames) only for "what's happening" questions.
  • Lowest resolution that works: small frames for scene questions, full 12 MP only for reading text.
  • Mic only when needed: the Bluetooth mic link opens during a session and closes after 60 seconds of silence. Interview, game and translator modes keep it open until you end them.
  • Listening options in the app: Always listening (wake word, most power), Smart (wake word only while a mode is active, otherwise tap to talk), Tap to talk (least power).
  • Auto disconnect when the glasses go in the case or nothing has happened for 10 minutes.

Phone

  • Voice activity detection: audio goes to the AI only while someone is speaking, which saves radio power and free-tier quota.
  • Close idle sessions: the Gemini Live connection closes after 2 minutes idle and reopens instantly on the next request.
  • Heavy work on the laptop: embeddings, game engines, research and code analysis run on the brain server, not the phone.
  • Duty-cycled sound awareness: YAMNet runs on short audio windows, and only when the feature is on.
  • Background done right: one foreground service for the glasses session; sync and memory indexing through WorkManager while charging; follows Android Doze.
  • Small responses: brief spoken answers by default, with details sent as text to the phone instead of long audio.
